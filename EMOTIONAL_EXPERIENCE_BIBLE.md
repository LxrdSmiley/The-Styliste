# The Styliste — Emotional Experience Bible

Status: **creator-approved production authority for GDD v8**  
Creator direction confirmed: July 25, 2026  
Decision-status rule: **creator-approved decisions are binding; labeled implementation recommendations remain provisional until tested or separately approved.**  
Primary audience: young adults  
World tone: realistic with heightened drama

## Emotional thesis

The player should feel that they built their own luxury fashion empire: a private point of view became visible, desirable, commercially real, culturally discussed, globally ambitious, and historically remembered.

Success must feel earned. Failure must feel consequential but recoverable. Glamour must feel like transformation, authorship, confidence, and arrival—not exclusion, spending, body judgment, or empty rarity.

## Emotional hierarchy

### Primary tone

**Aspirational and glamorous.**

### Secondary tones, strongest to weakest

1. **Mature and dramatic** — high-stakes creative and corporate crises, reputation, plagiarism, supply failure, rival strategy, and real consequences.
2. **Sharp and satirical** — Vex, trend-chasing, false exclusivity, commercial hypocrisy, and fashion-industry pretension.
3. **Competitive and intense** — Aurelian Gala, seasonal divisions, district control, and Maison Wars.
4. **Warm and character-led** — Luxe, mentors, staff, collaborators, and loyal customers.
5. **Mysterious and prestigious** — Aurelian identity, global capitals, archives, institutions, and the Hall of Sovereigns.
6. **Playful and humorous** — subtle NPC, trend, and character beats.
7. **Relaxing and comforting** — available through humane pacing and non-punitive offline play, but intentionally the weakest tone.

No screen, character, event, or marketing campaign may invert this hierarchy without explicit creator approval.

## World-emotion contract

The world is realistic with heightened drama. Craftsmanship, logistics, supply chains, retail, buyers, media, schools, subcultures, institutions, and business consequences remain credible. Narrative escalation makes decisions cinematic and emotionally sharp without becoming random soap opera, high-school drama, nihilism, or fantasy disconnected from the fashion industry.

## Core tensions

- Art versus commerce.
- Prestige versus accessibility.
- Control versus collaboration.
- Authenticity versus trend relevance.
- Growth versus integrity.
- Scarcity versus community.
- Ambition versus responsibility.
- Reinvention versus abandonment of identity.
- Local cultural authority versus global expansion.
- Glamour versus empty status performance.

These tensions create drama. Currency, Hype, and rank are evidence of consequences, not measures of human worth.

## Four pacing modes

### Work

Focused, tactile, competent. The player creates, sources, negotiates, prices, staffs, and solves operational problems.

### Anticipation

Samples, deliveries, buyer replies, release preparation, rivalry, and uncertainty create tension without coercive real-time pressure.

### Spectacle

Collection reveals, Gala, city arrivals, major rival encounters, and earned milestones receive cinematic emphasis. Spectacle remains rare enough to matter.

### Reflection

Luxe conversations, Archive, seasonal review, recovery, and quiet observation allow meaning, warmth, and personal ownership.

## Character-emotion roles

### Luxe

Luxe is the player’s personal assistant, fashion-industry insider, mascot, and first trusted professional relationship. Luxe protects the player from confusion, not consequence. Luxe is supportive, competent, honest, non-pushy, and adaptive. Luxe never becomes a sales bot, performs irreversible actions, or gives fake flattery.

### Vex

Vex is a respected critic whose sharpness creates productive exposure. Vex may be scathing about weak construction, false exclusivity, trend-chasing, hypocrisy, or an unearned price. Vex never attacks identity, body, protected traits, intelligence, or real-world worth. Growth receives precise acknowledgment.

### Seraphine Vale

Seraphine is the main persistent rival and strategic foil. The emotional relationship is dynamic rather than fixed hostility. Player choices can create escalating competition, grudging respect, strategic cooperation, temporary alliance, or deeper conflict. She never exists merely to humiliate the player.

## Failure and comeback

Failure changes the House story. Recovery requires diagnosis, sacrifice, altered behavior, relationship repair, and time. The player retains meaningful agency and at least two viable responses. The overall emotional direction remains aspirational rather than bleak.

## Emotional boundaries

- Do not use shame, body ideals, wealth, popularity, or prestige to define human worth.
- Do not drive spending through humiliation, crisis, social exclusion, or fear.
- Do not make serious issues preachy or mechanically one-sided; present ethical decisions as understandable tradeoffs with consequences.
- Do not turn heightened drama into morbid, cynical, or relentlessly dark content.
- Do not allow competitive intensity to become harassment, permanent caste, or coerced multiplayer labor.
- Preserve aspiration for ethical, accessible, community-focused, unconventional, and commercially ambitious Houses.

## Emotional completion test

After one session, the player should remember:

1. one creative or business choice they felt ownership over;
2. one uncertainty they genuinely considered;
3. one person who reacted for an understandable reason;
4. one visible consequence;
5. one glamorous or cinematic moment;
6. one reason to return that is not a timer, streak, or reward claim.
