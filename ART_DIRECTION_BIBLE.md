# The Styliste — Art Direction Bible

Status: **creator-approved production authority for GDD v8**  
Creator direction confirmed: July 25, 2026  
Decision-status rule: **creator-approved decisions are binding; labeled implementation recommendations remain provisional until tested or separately approved.**  
Direction: Aurelian Radiance  
Primary tone: aspirational and glamorous

## Art thesis

The Styliste must look like a globally authored luxury-fashion world in motion: editorial, cinematic, materially specific, culturally varied, and strategically legible. Luxury comes from proportion, craft, light, texture, confidence, and restraint—not indiscriminate gold, generic AI imagery, sexualization, or asset volume.

## Non-negotiable visual prohibitions

The product must not become:

- generic AI art, disconnected prompt output, stock luxury renders, or an asset dump;
- overly sexualized, trashy, body-shaming, or dependent on clickbait character design;
- childish, excessively cartoony, or visually equivalent to a children’s dress-up game;
- hyper-saturated casino UI or match-3 presentation;
- spreadsheet-only, sterile, or visually ugly on the Architect path;
- culturally flattened into one global luxury aesthetic;
- inconsistent across screens, characters, garments, cities, and marketing.

AI-assisted asset production, when used, must pass human art direction, consistency, anatomy, construction, cultural, legal, and provenance review. Unreviewed generation is not shippable art.

## Aurelian Radiance core

Aurelian Radiance is the unifying brand language across every city and style treatment.

### Core qualities

1. Aspirational and glamorous.
2. Editorial rather than casino-like.
3. Cinematic but readable on portrait mobile.
4. Fashion-led rather than generic fantasy.
5. Material-specific and construction-aware.
6. Mature rather than childish.
7. Controlled rather than visually noisy.
8. Culturally specific without stereotypes.
9. Beautiful in reduced-motion and low-resource modes.
10. Visually responsive to the player’s House identity and history.

## Core palette and materials

- **Obsidian:** executive depth, night, authority, and contrast.
- **Ivory:** editorial workspaces, reading, sketching, and creative clarity.
- **Antique gold:** earned emphasis, House identity, commitment, and ceremony.
- **Champagne:** aspiration, light, polish, and soft highlights.
- **Oxidized bronze:** history, machinery, archives, and industrial warmth.
- **Ink black:** typography, linework, criticism, construction, and Vex.
- **City accents:** researched local color, material, climate, and visual-culture accents that never replace the shared Aurelian core.

Gold is never used to disguise monetization, inflate ordinary rewards, or make every surface look prestigious.

## City visual dialect system

The game does not force one illustration style on every city. Each Production City receives an approved **visual dialect** derived from its fashion ecosystem, visual culture, architecture, media language, and narrative function.

The player’s House, the Aurelian UI, core information hierarchy, material rules, and character continuity remain consistent. City dialect affects environmental art, framing, linework, animation treatment, editorial cards, event presentation, and selected character/garment rendering—not authoritative gameplay clarity.

### Foundational dialects

**Creator-approved:** Kingston’s direction, Milan as stylized editorial, Tokyo as anime-influenced editorial, and the rule that city styles may differ while remaining part of one game.  
**Provisional production recommendations:** Paris, London, New York, and later-city dialect details below require creator and cultural review before production lock.

- **Kingston — cultural pulse and resourceful ambition:** cinematic street-to-studio energy, vibrant but controlled color, heat, movement, independent creators, custom tailoring, streetwear, music-subculture connections, markets, community validation, and visible commerce. Avoid beaches-as-identity, poverty spectacle, caricature, tourist-poster Jamaica, shallow dancehall shorthand, and “inferior starter city” framing.
- **Milan — stylized editorial:** sculptural silhouettes, disciplined layouts, atelier precision, industrial luxury, warm stone, metal, glass, restrained fashion illustration, and magazine-level composition.
- **Tokyo — anime-influenced editorial:** expressive silhouette, deliberate linework, precision, layered subculture, digital energy, controlled stylization, and cinematic anime influence without reducing Tokyo to neon clichés or youth caricatures.
- **Paris — couture illustration and institutional prestige:** fashion-illustration influence, controlled romanticism, archival linework, atelier intimacy, ceremony, and exacting detail without postcard clichés.
- **London — experimental mixed media:** collage, print, subculture, education, editorial disruption, weathered texture, and unconventional proportion without flattening the city into punk alone.
- **New York — cinematic commercial editorial:** verticality, speed, media, retail scale, celebrity styling, practical ambition, hard light, and graphic campaign language without treating commerce as cultural shallowness.

Later cities receive their own researched dialects. A city may combine stylized editorial, fashion illustration, anime influence, graphic realism, collage, or another approved treatment when it supports the city’s identity and remains technically feasible.

## Visual consistency contract

Different city dialects must still share:

- stable character identity and readable anatomy;
- the same House Codes and garment-authoring truth;
- consistent UI tokens and interaction states;
- material behavior that communicates weight and construction;
- compatible camera, portrait composition, and accessibility rules;
- clear commercial and narrative consequence presentation;
- restrained Aurelian ceremonial language.

Style variation must feel intentional, not like the project changed art teams without direction.

## Garment direction

Every garment must define:

- silhouette readable at phone size;
- construction logic and plausible assembly;
- fabric weight, movement, finish, and behavior;
- detail hierarchy;
- palette purpose;
- intended audience, use, and price logic;
- relationship to House Codes and collection narrative;
- styling possibilities;
- cultural references, consent, attribution, and review status.

No garment ships because it is rare, sexualized, excessively ornamented, or generated quickly. A smaller, curated catalogue is preferable to large volumes of incoherent fashion.

## Character direction

Characters are designed for young-adult audiences through fashion, attitude, competence, contradiction, and social presence—not sexual exploitation.

Every important character requires a recognizable silhouette, posture, movement, wardrobe logic, material identity, expression range, gesture vocabulary, and city/social context.

### Luxe

- Androgynous/fluid presentation.
- Aurelian editorial identity.
- Refined architectural tailoring, clean lines, sculptural silhouettes, and restrained metallic or monochrome accents.
- Wardrobe adapts subtly to the player’s House trajectory without losing Luxe’s identity.
- Visual warmth and competence; never a shop mascot or infantilized helper.

### Vex

- Respected critic, not tabloid villain.
- Sharp, graphic silhouette and controlled theatricality.
- Ink, paper, structured tailoring, and precise gestures.
- Authority comes from observation, memory, and standards.
- No visual coding that equates cruelty, disability, body type, age, or identity with villainy.

### Seraphine Vale

- Main rival and founder of Maison Vanta.
- Disciplined power, strategic control, and recognizable Vanta geometry.
- Visual relationship may shift with respect, escalation, alliance, or hostility while her core identity remains stable.
- Not designed as a one-note villain or sexualized rival stereotype.

## Atelier direction

The Atelier is tactile and visibly worked in: samples, paper, pins, threads, fabric rolls, tools, marks, versions, rejected attempts, and evidence of craft. It becomes more capable and personal over time without losing the feeling that fashion is made through hands, judgment, and revision.

## Architect and store direction

The Architect path must never become a sterile spreadsheet. Operational state is visualized through customer movement, store queues, stock visibility, deliveries, staff behavior, trade routes, renovation stages, sampling, production, and environmental change. Dense data remains available through progressive disclosure.

## UI direction

- Five stable destinations.
- One dominant player goal per screen.
- Editorial cards rather than casino tiles.
- Gold reserved for commitment, selected identity, and earned ceremony.
- No permanent wall of badges or simultaneous countdowns.
- Charts and receipts explain causes and consequences.
- Casual and Expert views share one authority model.
- Monetization remains visually secondary.

## Motion and cinematics

Motion expresses cloth, confidence, arrival, consequence, rivalry, and transformation. Avoid constant pulsing, bobbing, confetti, camera shake, or particle floods. Signature reveals use controlled camera movement, material close-ups, character interpretation, city context, and settled results.

## Marketing image rule

Every screenshot and key image must truthfully represent the actual build. It must communicate luxury fashion, House identity, human response, cultural context, and strategic consequence. Generic AI-rendered key art that cannot be reproduced by the game is prohibited.

## Art acceptance

An asset passes when it is:

- recognizably part of The Styliste;
- compatible with its city’s approved dialect;
- aspirational and glamorous without being cheap or exploitative;
- construction-, material-, and culture-aware;
- readable on the target phone;
- consistent with the character, House, and narrative state;
- reviewed by relevant human expertise when cultural specificity is involved.
