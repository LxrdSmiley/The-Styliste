## Audio thesis

The Styliste uses music, sound, silence, and haptics to connect cultural identity, craft, ambition, business, rivalry, ceremony, and memory. The soundtrack is not one generic luxury genre. It changes with city, scene, and fashion ecosystem while maintaining an Aurelian sonic identity.

It must not sound like generic hotel-lobby luxury, stock “world music,” casino reward design, continuous trailer music, or an imitation of any named artist, fashion house, game, or film.

## Aurelian sonic core

The shared sonic identity combines:

- sculptural rhythm;
- editorial confidence;
- warm low-end;
- precise transient detail;
- cinematic harmonic lift;
- controlled tension;
- material and spatial realism;
- restraint and meaningful silence.

This core remains recognizable as city-specific musical dialects change.

## Kingston musical identity

Kingston is the cultural and emotional home of the opening experience.

Its sound may draw from:

- dancehall and reggae subcultures;
- contemporary Jamaican production language;
- Afrobeats connections where contextually appropriate;
- modern R&B;
- bass-heavy street and club energy;
- sound-system spatiality;
- warm community, market, studio, tailoring, and city ambience.

Production requires Jamaican musical and cultural consultation. Avoid stock reggae loops, tourist music, indiscriminate patois samples, caricatured sound effects, or treating Jamaican influence as background flavor detached from fashion and community.

## Global runway and luxury identity

High-fashion and global-scale moments may combine:

- runway house and deep house;
- polished electronic music;
- orchestral couture;
- cinematic pop;
- ambient electronic textures;
- editorial percussion;
- city-specific instrumentation and production language.

Aurelian Gala, major collection reveals, territory conflict, and global-city arrivals may become more kinetic and ceremonial without sounding like a slot-machine jackpot.

## Reference landmarks

The following are reference categories for mood and production analysis, not imitation or licensing permission:

- the dramatic electronic and sculptural tension of major luxury runway presentations;
- the urban-chic jazz, hip-hop, and electronic confidence associated with *Persona 5*;
- the atmospheric electronic world-building associated with *Cyberpunk 2077*;
- the opulent tension associated with *The Neon Demon* and *House of Gucci*;
- contemporary Jamaican fashion films, documentaries, studio culture, dancehall, and street-culture presentation.

No melody, recording, arrangement, trademarked sonic identifier, or protected performance may be copied.

## City sonic dialects

Each Production City requires a researched sonic brief covering music, ambience, spaces, fashion events, transport, retail, workrooms, nightlife, and cultural review.

City music may change genre and production style, but important gameplay functions retain consistent sound families so players understand selection, warning, commitment, success, and failure across the world.

## Character motifs

### Luxe

Luminous, warm, precise, adaptive, and androgynous in sonic presentation. Luxe’s motif should feel like competent arrival and intelligent support rather than magic, cuteness, or sales pressure.

### Vex

Ink-like attack, clipped editorial rhythm, controlled theatrical sting, and professional authority. Vex’s sound signals scrutiny, not villainy or abuse.

### Seraphine Vale / Maison Vanta

Geometric, disciplined, strategic, and controlled. Harmonic warmth or friction may evolve with respect, alliance, rivalry, and conflict while preserving a recognizable Vanta motif.

Character motifs should be recognizable in one to three seconds and have visual/haptic equivalents.

## Material interaction library

Define distinct sound families for silk, cotton, wool, leather, denim, synthetics, embellishment, metal hardware, paper patterns, scissors, pins, thread, sewing, pressing, packaging, racks, doors, workshop machinery, showrooms, and retail surfaces.

Sounds communicate material weight, finish, tension, and state—not merely that the player tapped.

## Functional language

- **Selection:** immediate, elegant, low-fatigue.
- **Commitment:** weighted, final, and House-specific.
- **Success:** aspirational lift without payout language.
- **Warning:** clear, mature, and non-punitive.
- **Error:** specific and recoverable.
- **Ledger:** precise, restrained, and trustworthy.
- **Relationship change:** human and contextual.
- **Reveal:** anticipation, material focus, expansion, then consequence.
- **Rival move:** strategic pressure rather than horror sting.
- **Archive:** memory, paper, distance, and legacy.

## Haptics

- Light selection: single subtle tick.
- Confirmed commitment: short weighted pulse.
- Warning: restrained double pulse.
- Major reveal: optional layered pulse.
- Error: distinct but not harsh.
- Rival intervention: controlled directional or patterned pulse when supported.

Respect device settings and provide independent control.

## Silence rules

Silence or near-silence is used for concentrated Atelier work, reading Vex, serious crisis decisions, emotional Luxe scenes, and Archive reflection. The game must not fill every surface with looping music.

## Accessibility and performance

- Independent music, effects, voice, ambience, and haptic controls.
- Captions or transcripts for spoken and meaningful audio content.
- Visual equivalents for essential cues.
- No rhythm-only required interaction.
- Dynamic-range and reduced-intensity options.
- Low-resource mode limits voices, layers, reverb, and simultaneous effects without removing state information.
- Audio memory, streaming, and latency budgets must be measured on the reference Android device.
