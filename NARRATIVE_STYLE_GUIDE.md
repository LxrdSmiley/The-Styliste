# The Styliste — Narrative Style Guide

Status: **creator-approved production authority for GDD v8**  
Creator direction confirmed: July 25, 2026  
Decision-status rule: **creator-approved decisions are binding; labeled implementation recommendations remain provisional until tested or separately approved.**  
Primary audience: young adults  
World tone: realistic with heightened drama

## Voice thesis

The writing is aspirational, glamorous, mature, fashion-literate, character-led, culturally respectful, mobile-readable, and economically understandable. It presents heightened drama grounded in actual fashion-industry decisions.

It must be sharp without cruelty, glamorous without purple prose, dramatic without soap-opera trash, ethical without preaching, and humorous without becoming childish.

## Tone hierarchy

1. Mature and dramatic.
2. Sharp and satirical.
3. Competitive and intense.
4. Warm and character-led.
5. Mysterious and prestigious.
6. Playful and humorous.
7. Relaxing and comforting.

## Core writing rules

1. Refer to actual player choices, settled facts, and legitimate character knowledge.
2. Use concrete nouns and verbs before adjectives.
3. Keep mobile dialogue concise; one beat carries one main idea.
4. Explain commercial consequences in human language without removing professional texture.
5. Distinguish characters through values, rhythm, vocabulary, attention, and memory.
6. Let glamour emerge from confidence, specificity, aspiration, and consequence.
7. Ground crises in industry tradeoffs, craft, logistics, reputation, contracts, or relationships.
8. Preserve ambiguity in artistic interpretation; remove ambiguity when the player needs operational clarity.
9. Never invent achievements, motives, relationships, harm, or private information.
10. Do not make any city or culture sound like a stereotype or aesthetic resource node.

## Prohibited writing

- Fake flattery or unearned praise.
- Generic motivational dialogue.
- Repetitive “iconic,” “legendary,” “luxury,” or “slay” language.
- Machine-like adjective piles.
- Fake youth slang or trend-chasing dialect.
- Phonetic accents used as characterization.
- Shallow melodrama, random betrayal, or high-school conflict detached from the fashion industry.
- Relentless bleakness, morbidity, or cynical nihilism.
- Heavy-handed political lecturing.
- Incorrect fashion, textile, production, retail, media, or finance terminology.
- Personal insults, body shaming, identity attacks, or protected-trait commentary.
- Characters referencing events they could not know.

## Luxe voice

Luxe is the player’s personal assistant, fashion-industry insider, mascot, strategic guide, and first trusted professional relationship.

Luxe is:

- supportive and competent;
- grounded and honest;
- protective of the House’s emerging identity;
- non-pushy and commercially neutral;
- adaptive to the House’s history and the player’s preferred guidance density;
- capable of warmth without false reassurance.

Luxe speaks from verified game state. Luxe may identify a mistake, bottleneck, contradiction, or risk, then present recoverable options. Luxe never performs an irreversible action, forces a decision, or becomes a notification/shop bot.

Typical rhythm: **observation → meaning → options or next decision**.

## Vex voice

Vex is a respected elite critic whose authority comes from standards, observation, memory, and independence.

Vex evaluates:

- originality;
- silhouette;
- construction and material execution;
- restraint;
- cultural relevance and context;
- pricing and commercial integrity;
- House claims and hypocrisy;
- repetition, revision, and growth.

Vex may be scathing, blunt, witty, and unsympathetic toward weak work. Vex never becomes abusive, tabloid-like, body-shaming, identity-based, or randomly hostile. Every harsh verdict is evidence-based and inspectable. Genuine improvement receives precise respect.

Typical rhythm: **verdict → evidence → implication**.

## Seraphine Vale voice

Seraphine is the player’s main persistent rival and founder of Maison Vanta. She is a strategic foil rather than a static villain.

Her dialogue reveals:

- commercial and creative strategy;
- disciplined ambition;
- changing respect or contempt;
- credible self-interest;
- memory of the player’s ethics, pricing, positioning, crisis behavior, and competitive choices;
- possible cooperation when interests align.

She may challenge, provoke, recruit, negotiate, cooperate, or escalate. She does not exist solely to insult or sabotage.

## Customer and community voices

Customers speak from taste, budget, use, identity, memory, community, and circumstance. Named customers may disagree with their simulation cohort. Jamaican and other city-specific voices require informed authorship or review; no character is reduced to catchphrases.

## Commercial and institutional voices

Buyers, manufacturers, suppliers, retailers, stylists, journalists, schools, museums, staff, regulators, and institutions communicate clear stakes and professional constraints. Their language may remain specialized where context teaches meaning.

## Satire boundary

Satire targets systems, pretension, hypocrisy, trend-chasing, false scarcity, and commercial contradiction. It does not target marginalized identities, bodies, poverty, accent, culture, or player worth.

## Dialogue length targets

- Tooltip: 4–12 words.
- Button label: 1–4 words.
- Result cause: 6–18 words.
- Luxe guidance beat: 12–40 words.
- Vex verdict beat: 10–35 words.
- Conversation message: usually under 60 words.
- Major narrative card: 60–160 words with optional expansion.

These are usability targets, not rigid literary limits.

## Evidence-grounded dynamic writing

Every dynamic line references structured evidence such as `design.material`, `result.stockout`, `house_code.price_accessibility`, `relationship.buyer_trust`, `rival.seraphine_respect`, or `history.collection_repetition`.

Generated prose may rephrase approved evidence. It cannot create facts, scores, motives, identities, or authoritative outcomes.

## Writing acceptance

A line passes when readers can identify who said it, what evidence caused it, what it reveals about that character, and what it means for the player. It fails when it could be copied into any fashion game unchanged.
