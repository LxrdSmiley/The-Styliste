# The Styliste — Product Positioning Bible

Status: **creator-approved production authority for GDD v8**  
Creator direction confirmed: July 25, 2026  
Decision-status rule: **creator-approved decisions are binding; labeled implementation recommendations remain provisional until tested or separately approved.**  
Primary audience: young adults  
Primary tone: aspirational and glamorous

## Core player fantasy

The primary player takeaway is:

> **I built my own luxury fashion empire.**

Every public description, first-session promise, trailer, store page, and major milestone must reinforce that fantasy. The player is not merely styling an avatar, collecting clothing, operating an idle counter, or watching a fashion story. The player creates a House, establishes its identity, builds the business around it, expands through the global fashion world, and lives with the consequences of its decisions.

## Category position

**The Styliste is a luxury fashion empire simulation with strategy-drama structure.**

The internal positioning phrase **fashion-house strategy drama** remains useful for design and production because it describes the connection between creative choices, business strategy, relationships, rivalry, and remembered consequences. It is not required to be the first phrase used in player-facing marketing.

Recommended player-facing promise:

> Create a House. Define its point of view. Build the luxury fashion empire the world remembers.

## Competitive lane

The Styliste will not attempt to defeat every competitor at its strongest specialty. It does not need the largest wardrobe, biggest open world, fastest idle growth, most frequent events, broadest social network, or highest photorealistic fidelity.

It competes through:

1. A complete luxury-fashion-empire fantasy spanning art and business.
2. Recognizable personal authorship through House Codes and collection history.
3. Fashion-industry literacy across design, production, retail, media, culture, and institutions.
4. Consequences that are visible, explainable, and remembered.
5. Recurring characters with evidence-based reactions and changing relationships.
6. Mature, heightened drama without shallow soap-opera conflict.
7. Fair strategy and F2P competitive parity.
8. A global city framework in which each city has its own cultural and visual dialect.

## Audience

The primary audience is **young adults** who are interested in one or more of the following:

- fashion, styling, illustration, design, culture, or personal expression;
- tycoon, simulation, strategy, management, and progression games;
- glamorous character drama and persistent rivalries;
- building a recognizable identity rather than collecting disconnected items;
- solo-first play with optional social and competitive depth.

The exact platform age rating remains a legal, content, and storefront decision. Young-adult targeting does not authorize sexualized, exploitative, predatory, or age-inappropriate presentation.

## Emotional position

### Primary tone

**Aspirational and glamorous.**

The player should feel ambition, transformation, confidence, desire, prestige, ceremony, creative authorship, and the satisfaction of building something globally significant.

### Secondary-tone hierarchy

1. **Mature and dramatic** — industry crises, persistent rivalry, plagiarism, reputation, supply failure, and consequential decisions.
2. **Sharp and satirical** — Vex, trend-chasing, false exclusivity, commercial hypocrisy, and fashion-industry pretension.
3. **Competitive and intense** — Gala, seasonal divisions, district control, and Maison Wars, with no P2W ceiling.
4. **Warm and character-led** — Luxe, mentors, staff, collaborators, and loyal customers.
5. **Mysterious and prestigious** — Aurelian identity, institutions, fashion capitals, archives, and the Hall of Sovereigns.
6. **Playful and humorous** — subtle character and trend beats, never the dominant tone.
7. **Relaxing and comforting** — present through voluntary pacing and non-punitive offline play, but intentionally the weakest secondary tone.

## World position

The world is **realistic with heightened drama**.

Real fashion-industry trade, craftsmanship, production, logistics, retail, media, schools, subcultures, and institutions provide the foundation. Persistent rivals, corporate crises, critics, territory competition, and cinematic presentation heighten the reality without turning it into fantasy nonsense or shallow melodrama.

## Global cultural promise

The game deliberately includes different cultures, backgrounds, fashion movements, and cities. No one city, culture, body, class, or fashion tradition represents the universal standard of taste.

Each city may use a distinct visual dialect—such as anime influence in Tokyo or stylized editorial presentation in Milan—while remaining recognizably part of The Styliste through shared Aurelian interface, material quality, consequence language, and House continuity.

## Five-second hook

> **Build your own luxury fashion empire.**

## Thirty-second trailer structure

1. Name the House and establish its visual identity.
2. Create or revise one meaningful garment detail.
3. Confront one production or commercial compromise.
4. Reveal a coherent capsule collection.
5. Show customers, a buyer, Luxe, Vex, and Seraphine reacting differently.
6. Show stores, stock, reputation, and opportunity changing in the world.
7. End on the next strategic decision and the promise of global expansion.

All footage must represent the actual live build.

## Store-page truth rules

- Lead with the luxury fashion empire fantasy.
- Show real interaction, not only cinematics.
- Do not imply garment freedom, cities, social systems, or character fidelity absent from the live build.
- Do not disguise tycoon and strategy play as pure dress-up.
- Do not present spending as talent, status, competence, or competitive power.
- Do not use generic AI art, stock luxury imagery, or visual assets that conflict with the actual product.
- Use actual portrait UI and representative device capture.

## Brand language

Preferred terms: luxury fashion empire, House, collection, point of view, craft, atelier, production, buyer, audience, reputation, legacy, archive, reinvention, glamour, strategy, culture, city, industry.

Avoid as primary descriptors: addictive, grind, dominate, clicker, instant fame, unlimited luxury, dress-up only, idle rewards, casino, collect them all.

## Positioning acceptance

Positioning passes when unprompted young-adult testers say some recognizable version of:

> “I built my own luxury fashion empire.”

It fails when most testers describe the game only as a dress-up app, idle clicker, spreadsheet, generic social-fashion game, or disconnected collection of menus.
